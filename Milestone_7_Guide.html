1. The Professional Principle: Never Hide Poor Predictions

In academic and industrial engineering, a machine learning model is only trustworthy if engineers know where it excels and where it fails. Presenting only aggregate scores ($R^2 = 0.92$) hides critical edge-case risks.

Why Did the Model Underperform on These Mixes? (Root Cause Analysis)

  1. The Ultra-High Strength Boundary Phenomenon (>70 MPa):
    Notice that 4 of the 5 worst errors are ultra-high-strength mixes (74 to 82 MPa), and in every single case, the model underpredicted by 14 to 24 MPa.
  2. Algorithmic Limitation (Tree Leaf Averaging):
    In Decision Trees and Gradient Boosters, predictions inside a leaf node are calculated as the mean of training samples falling into that partition. In our dataset, mixes above 70 MPa represent only ~5% of all samples. Because training samples in this extreme tail are sparse, the highest terminal leaf averages out around 55–62 MPa. Tree models cannot extrapolate beyond this ceiling.
  3. Practical Engineering Takeaway:
    The model is extraordinarily reliable for normal and standard structural concrete (15 to 55 MPa, where 95%+ of construction occurs). For specialized ultra-high-performance concrete (>70 MPa), engineers should apply a conservative safety factor or provide additional training batches.
