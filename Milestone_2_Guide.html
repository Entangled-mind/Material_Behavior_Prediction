Complete Technical Guide to Milestone 2: Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)

1. Overview of Milestone 2 (EDA)

Exploratory Data Analysis is not about generating dozens of random plots. In machine learning for physical sciences, EDA serves two vital purposes:

  1. Statistical Validation: Identifying distribution shapes, skewness, outliers, and collinearity to choose appropriate preprocessing and modeling algorithms.
  2. Physical Law Verification: Confirming whether experimental observations obey known materials science principles (such as Abrams' Law and hydration kinetics).

4 04_water_cement_ratio_vs_strength.png • Abrams' Law Verification

Question Answered: Does the empirical dataset obey Abrams' Law (1918), the cornerstone law of concrete physics?

Key Findings:

  • Abrams' Law states: $ ext{Strength} = rac{A}{B^{w/c}}$, meaning compressive strength falls exponentially as the water-to-cement ratio ($w/c$) increases.
  • Our plot confirms a pronounced non-linear drop: as $w/c$ increases from 0.3 to 1.2, strength drops from >70 MPa down to <20 MPa.
  • Color-coding by curing age reveals that for any identical $w/c$ ratio, older specimens (56–365 days) consistently exhibit higher strength due to prolonged hydration.

3. Key Takeaways for Milestone 3 (Preprocessing)

Direct Guidance for Preprocessing & Modeling
  1. Feature Scaling is Essential: Feature ranges vary by orders of magnitude (age: 1–365, coarseagg: 800–1150, superplastic: 0–32). Distance-sensitive models (Linear Regression, KNN, Ridge) require scaling (StandardScaler).
  2. Non-Linearity Requires Tree-Based Models: The inverse relationship in Abrams' Law and the logarithmic hydration curve in curing age are non-linear. Linear regression will establish a baseline, but Decision Trees and Random Forests will capture these physical curves much more effectively.
  3. Zero Handling: The zeros in slag, ash, and superplasticizer are physically meaningful (absence of admixture) and must be preserved as 0, not treated as missing values.
