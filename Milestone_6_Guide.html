3. Key Scientific Discoveries & Verification

1 The "Big Three" Features Govern ~75% of Material Strength

Together, age (37.3%), cement (28.9%), and water (9.6%) account for 75.8% of the total predictive power of the model.

Why this makes total material science sense:
Civil engineering literature for over a century has established that compressive strength is predominantly dictated by the Water-to-Cement ratio ($w/c$) and the Curing Age ($t$). The machine learning model independently converged upon this exact physical conclusion without any human prompting.

2 Why Did 'Age' Leap to #1 in Tree Ensembles?

In Milestone 4 (Linear Regression), cement was ranked as the strongest predictor (+12.12), while age was a distant third (+6.89). But in Gradient Boosting, age surged to the #1 most critical feature (37.3%).

The Mathematical & Physical Reason:

  • Concrete hydration is logarithmic: a specimen gains 70% of its strength in the first 28 days, then plateaus.
  • A linear model can only apply a single rigid slope across all 365 days, severely under-representing the dramatic impact of early curing.
  • Tree ensembles can split age at multiple non-linear thresholds (age ≤ 7, age ≤ 14, age ≤ 28), unlocking the full non-linear kinetics of hydration!

3 Test Set Permutation Importance Confirms Generalization

In visualizations/12_permutation_importance_test.png, we randomly scrambled each column on the 201 unseen test samples:

  • Scrambling age causes test $R^2$ to collapse by 0.71 (dropping from 0.92 down to 0.21!).
  • Scrambling cement collapses test $R^2$ by 0.54.
  • Scrambling water collapses test $R^2$ by 0.18.

This proves that the model's test performance is genuine and anchored directly in the physical chemistry of the ingredients.
